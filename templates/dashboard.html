About the Algorithm
In-depth explanation of the NLP pipeline and machine learning methodology.
NLP Preprocessing Pipeline
1. Lowercase Conversion "WIN" → "win"
2. URL Replacement URLs → [url] token
3. Punctuation Removal Remove @#$%! etc.
4. Tokenisation (NLTK) Split into word tokens
5. Stopword Removal Remove "the", "is", "a"…
6. Lemmatisation (WordNet) "winning" → "win"
TF-IDF Explained
TF-IDF Formula TF-IDF(t, d) = TF(t, d) × IDF(t)
IDF(t) = log(N / df(t))
Example: In an email containing 100 words where "free" appears 5 times:
TF("free") = 5/100 = 0.05
IDF("free") = log(5574/600) ≈ 2.23
TF-IDF = 0.05 × 2.23 = 0.112
Words rare across the corpus but frequent in one email get high TF-IDF — key spam signal!
Multinomial Naive Bayes
Bayes' Theorem P(Spam | Email) = P(Email | Spam) × P(Spam) / P(Email)
Prior P(Spam): ~13.4% of emails in training are spam.
Likelihood P(Email|Spam): Product of each word's spam probability.
Posterior P(Spam|Email): Final classification probability.

The "naive" assumption: all words are independent given the class. Despite being unrealistic, it works extremely well in practice.
Why Naive Bayes Works for Spam
Fast training and inference — scales linearly with feature count.
Handles high-dimensional sparse data — exactly what TF-IDF produces.
Probabilistic output — gives interpretable confidence scores.
>98% accuracy on the SMS Spam Collection benchmark dataset.
Resistant to overfitting — works well with limited labelled data.
